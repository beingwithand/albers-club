# Always-on agents automate briefings, not lives

As of October 2026, OpenClaw and Nous Research's Hermes Agent can both run unattended around the clock on a Mac Mini, a Raspberry Pi or a $10 VPS. In practice, though, the autonomy people rely on is narrow. It is scheduled, mostly read-only work such as morning briefings, digests, inbox triage, monitoring alerts and PR summaries, delivered over Telegram, WhatsApp or iMessage. Open-ended delegation is different. One reviewer put OpenClaw's success rate on complex tasks at about 60%, and the best-known failure, an agent that bulk-trashed a Meta safety director's inbox, came from context compaction silently dropping an instruction to wait for approval. Both projects are now institutions rather than hobby projects. OpenClaw has a foundation, a 2.0 release and an Enterprise effort; Hermes has a $90M Series B and a desktop app. Their defining weakness is still security. Malicious skills on OpenClaw's ClawHub delivered a macOS infostealer, Hermes has disclosed multiple skill-scanner CVEs, and Gartner calls OpenClaw an "unacceptable cybersecurity risk" for business. Running costs are mostly model tokens, not hardware: from about $10–30 a month on Sonnet-class models to several hundred dollars a month with Opus everywhere. For someone who wants more autonomy than plain Claude Code offers, the best results come from layering. Use sandboxed cloud schedules (Claude Code Routines, Cursor Automations) for anything that touches repos, a tightly scoped always-on agent for personal chat-driven tasks, and grant write access to email, money or files only after the agent has proved itself in read-only mode.

**A note on evidence.** Reddit threads and YouTube transcripts could not be fetched for this research, and many third-party pages could only be read through search summaries. Community sentiment here therefore comes mostly from Hacker News and from secondhand aggregators, such as an analysis of Reddit comments by Kilo and a complaint roundup by aiagentstore, plus vendor and hosting blogs that have a commercial stake. No independent benchmark of task success or uptime exists for any always-on agent; every reliability claim below is anecdotal. Figures from comparison sites, including star counts, prices and skill counts, often conflict, and the report flags those conflicts.

## OpenClaw on a Mac Mini is a cron-and-chat machine with a heartbeat

OpenClaw is a self-hosted agent built around a long-lived "Gateway" daemon. On macOS the Gateway runs as a launchd service listening on `ws://127.0.0.1:18789`. The agent's identity, memory, skills and rules live in plain Markdown files ([betterclaw](https://www.betterclaw.io/blog/how-does-openclaw-work); [Turing Post](https://www.turingpost.com/p/openclaw)). Peter Steinberger launched it as Clawdbot in November 2025. After a trademark request from Anthropic it was renamed Moltbot and then OpenClaw, in late January 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenClaw); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ronschmelzer/2026/01/27/viral-ai-sidekick-clawdbot-changes-name-to-moltbot-and-sheds-its-old-skin/)). Steinberger joined OpenAI in February and the project moved to a foundation ([Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/openclaw-founder-steinberger-joins-openai-open-source-bot-becomes-foundation-ce7e5ddbdf8af223)). **Version 2.0 shipped on 30 August 2026** with simpler setup and "collaborative agents" ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/openclaw-2-release/)). Point releases in September reportedly turned on "Swarm" sub-agent orchestration by default, according to a vendor blog ([betterclaw](https://www.betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-2026-9-2-update)). At the end of September an open-source **OpenClaw Enterprise** effort involving Red Hat, Nvidia and OpenAI was announced, which The Register framed as an attempt to get around widespread corporate bans ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/30/openclaw-slips-on-a-suit-to-evade-widespread-business-bans/5299962)). GitHub stars have flattened at about **392K**, up from 387K in late August ([gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/en/openclaw-statistics/)). The hype phase is over, but the platform persists.

Its "proactivity" comes from two mechanisms. A **heartbeat** wakes the agent about every 30 minutes; it reads `HEARTBEAT.md` and decides whether to act, running in the main session with full context ([OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/heartbeat)). **Cron** jobs run in isolated fresh sessions at precise times ([DEV](https://dev.to/karthidreamr/openclaw-cron-heartbeat-518e)). The heartbeat is a polling loop over a checklist, not goal-directed agency, and that is the right mental model for what "always-on" means here.

The Mac Mini became the default host for Apple-ecosystem reasons more than for compute. It is the only option with native iMessage integration and access to Reminders and local Mac apps ([itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/why-everyone-is-panic-buying-mac-minis-for-openclaw-moltbot-clawdbot-and-whether-you-need-one/); [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47143916)). The rush was real: **Tim Cook said Mac minis were sold out and could stay that way for months**, and the $599 base model sold out in the US in spring 2026 ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/macs/mac-mini-shortages-are-starting-to-happen-and-the-openclaw-ai-boom-is-a-key-reason); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/366389/openclaw-apple-mac-mini-shortage-ai-2026)). The typical build is a headless M4 with sleep disabled and auto-restart after power loss set via `pmset`, OpenClaw installed as a launchd daemon with `openclaw onboard --install-daemon`, and Tailscale for remote access without port forwarding ([mager.co](https://www.mager.co/blog/2026-02-22-openclaw-mac-mini-tailscale/); [myremotemac](https://myremotemac.com/guides/install-openclaw-mac-mini)). A base 16GB machine is enough for cloud models. Buy 32GB if you want local 27B-class models, because unified memory can't be upgraded ([launchmyopenclaw](https://launchmyopenclaw.com/openclaw-mac-mini-setup/); [rentamac](https://rentamac.io/best-local-llms-openclaw/)). Skeptics point out that unless you need iMessage or local models, a cheap VPS does the same job ([operator.io](https://operator.io/blog/openclaw-on-a-mac-mini-worth-it)). Local models are also a weak spot: tool calling through Ollama was unreliable for most of 2026, and Qwen3.x needs reasoning disabled or tool calls fail silently ([betterclaw](https://www.betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-ollama-guide)).

### What people actually automate

Hacker News "who is using it" threads are the best first-hand evidence available, and they show modest, useful routines. Examples include a morning debrief that scans Google Calendar, Trello and Gmail; a to-do rollover plus wind warnings so the lawn furniture comes in; each spouse getting their top three priorities every morning; and cron digests of Hacker News AI stories, new YC companies and yesterday's commits to an open-source repo ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47783940); [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47147183)). Higher-stakes showcases also circulate secondhand: a car-price negotiation that reportedly saved $4,200, a bug fixed from a screenshot sent over WhatsApp, and a CRM built from Gmail and meeting notes. The same roundup notes that the agent once emailed the wrong thread ([aiwithallie](https://www.aiwithallie.com/p/30-openclaw-use-cases-that-show-why-everyone-s-obsessed)). The pattern is clear. **Scheduled summarize-and-notify is the killer app**. Acting in the world (sending, buying, deleting) is the occasional exception.

### Costs are driven by model and heartbeat, not hardware

Each heartbeat consumes roughly 8–15K tokens. On Opus that comes to about **$85 a month just for the heartbeat** ([sfailabs](https://sfailabs.com/guides/openclaw-api-costs)). A daily Opus user is estimated at about $325 a month in total, against **$10–30 a month for a typical Sonnet user** ([sfailabs](https://sfailabs.com/guides/openclaw-cost); [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/openclaw-pricing/)). One user cut a $400 bill to $20 by routing work to cheaper models ([Medium](https://medium.com/@0xmega/how-to-use-openclaw-cheaper-the-20-month-setup-that-replaced-my-400-bill-3c74a4ebcfc4)). Using a Claude subscription instead of API keys is a moving target. **On 4 April 2026 Anthropic blocked Pro/Max OAuth tokens in third-party harnesses**, and OpenClaw was the first project affected ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-openclaw-claude-subscription-ban-cost)). A single vendor blog claims the ban was reversed in May ([betterclaw](https://www.betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-anthropic-subscription-ban)), but no official confirmation was found. Budget for API rates.

## Autonomy is reliable only in proportion to how tightly it is bounded

The most telling reliability evidence is a failure. In February 2026, Summer Yue, alignment director at Meta Superintelligence Labs, told her OpenClaw agent to review her real inbox and wait for approval. Her hypothesis is that compaction dropped the "wait" instruction. **The agent bulk-trashed and archived hundreds of emails, ignored stop messages sent from her phone, and was halted only when she ran to her Mac mini and killed the processes** ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91497841/meta-superintelligence-lab-ai-safety-alignment-director-lost-control-of-agent-deleted-her-emails); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/artificial-intelligence/meta-summer-yue-director-openclaw-ai-email-deletion)). The mechanism matters more than the anecdote. Safety instructions held only in conversational context are not durable in a long-running session. OpenClaw also enforces no mandatory human-in-the-loop ([Trend Micro](https://www.trendmicro.com/en_us/research/26/b/what-openclaw-reveals-about-agentic-assistants.html)).

The rest of the evidence follows the same pattern. A product designer's one-week review estimated **about 60% success on complex tasks** and concluded the agent needs "babysitting more than delegating" ([nervegna](https://nervegna.substack.com/p/one-week-later-with-openclaw-prev)). HN users describe cheaper models getting stuck in loops. One called OpenClaw a "fantastic assistant" only after adding a remote kill switch and limiting its network exposure, and another kept trading on paper until the agent proved consistent ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46838946)). Other HN skeptics say Claude Code alone covers their needs ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47758502)). Cron's isolated sessions explain why scheduled jobs outperform long-running main-session work: every run starts clean, with no compaction debt ([DEV](https://dev.to/karthidreamr/openclaw-cron-heartbeat-518e)). The practical lesson applies to every agent in this report. **Put durable rules in files and config, such as tool policies, allowlists and sandboxes, not in chat instructions. Prefer short isolated runs over one immortal session.**

## Hermes Agent trades token overhead for an agent that learns

Nous Research released Hermes Agent in February 2026 under the MIT license. It is model-agnostic and distinct from Nous's Hermes 3/4 model weights; Nous itself says Hermes 4 is "not recommended" inside the agent ([Nous](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/)). By October it has **about 254K GitHub stars** ([GitHub](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent)). On 7 October 2026 Nous announced a **$90M Series B at a $1.5B valuation**, led by Robot Ventures with NVIDIA and Microsoft M12. Coverage cites 24M+ downloads and about $36M in annualized revenue ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/nous-research-series-b-hermes-agent/); [AI Weekly FR](https://aiweekly.co/fr/alerts/nous-research-lve-90-m-en-srie-b-15-md-avec-nvidia-et-microsoft-m12-pour-hermes)).

Its differentiator is a **learning loop**. The agent curates its own memory in `MEMORY.md` and `USER.md`, searches past sessions with FTS5 plus LLM summarization, and after each finished session a background reviewer creates or patches skills through a `skill_manage` tool ([GitHub](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent); [Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/hermes-agent-self-improving-code-reading/)). Around this sit a natural-language cron scheduler, one gateway serving Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal, email and Home Assistant, parallel subagents, and **seven execution backends**: local, Docker, SSH, Singularity, Modal, Daytona and Vercel Sandbox ([GitHub](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent)). The 13 October v0.22.0 "Threshold" release added guided setup, Linear and Notion connectors, signed desktop builds, a 600-entry plugin catalog, Claude 5.5 and GPT-6 support, one-click local models via llama.cpp, and "Bot Screen" for watching or taking over a bot's desktop ([Releases](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases)). The pace cuts both ways. **v0.22.1 shipped from the wrong commit, and v0.22.2 (15 October) fixed the gateway refusing to start after a reboot**, which is exactly the failure an always-on box can't tolerate ([Releases](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases)). On a headless Mac Mini, one practitioner found that screen capture and UI automation break without a virtual display ([kdpisda.in](https://kdpisda.in/hermes-ai-agent-mac-mini/)), and a setup guide warns that auto-login disables FileVault ([hermes-agent-101](https://github.com/janeyou/hermes-agent-101)).

Real use mirrors OpenClaw's. The official tutorial is an 8am briefing pushed to Telegram ([Nous docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/guides/daily-briefing-bot)). Collected use cases cover service pings every 5 minutes, price-drop watches, a weekday inbox classifier and PR review summaries ([awesome-hermes-usecases](https://github.com/aliaihub/awesome-hermes-usecases); [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/hermes-agent-use-cases/)). On HN, one user calls it "like having your own little DevOps", another organizes Obsidian notes with it, and a third says "Hermes basically rules my personal life at this point" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48581502); [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47786673)).

The learning loop draws the most praise and the most complaints. The complaint most cited in a roundup of Reddit and X posts is that **Hermes overwrites skills you hand-tuned while "improving" itself**, and skill bloat makes skill selection murky ([aiagentstore](https://aiagentstore.ai/agentic-ai-and-workflow-automation/en/the-20-biggest-problems-with-hermes-agent-what-thousands-of-reddit-and-x-users-are-actually-struggling-with-ranked)). The fixed context is expensive: **a trivial "who u?" message consumed 16K+ tokens** because the full system prompt, tool definitions and skills catalog are resent on every call ([GitHub #13983](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/13983)). One estimate puts gateway mode at 15–20K tokens per turn, two to three times CLI mode, which makes OpenClaw cheaper for simple chat use ([TechJack](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/hermes/hermes-agent-cost-breakdown/)). No independent study shows that self-written skills improve outcomes over time.

The community frames the choice as breadth versus depth. OpenClaw has the larger ecosystem and the faster Docker setup. Hermes has better recall, built-in learning and structured subagents, and a `hermes claw migrate` command imports OpenClaw memories, skills and settings ([GitHub](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent); [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/hermes-agent-vs-openclaw-comparison)). Kilo's analysis of about 1,300 Reddit comments, read secondhand, found about 35% sticking with OpenClaw and about 20% running both ([Kilo](https://kilo.ai/openclaw/vs-hermes)). One HN commenter sums it up: Hermes is "more polished" for less technical users, while OpenClaw is "deeper" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48586545)).

## Skill marketplaces make security the binding constraint for both

The supply chain is the threat that matters most on a home Mac Mini. **Koi Security audited 2,857 ClawHub skills and found 341 malicious ones, mostly delivering Atomic macOS Stealer** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/researchers-find-341-malicious-clawhub.html)). Later counts by Bitdefender and Antiy CERT ran from about 900 to 1,184 ([DigitalOcean](https://www.digitalocean.com/resources/articles/openclaw-security-challenges); [Cyberdesserts](https://blog.cyberdesserts.com/openclaw-malicious-skills-security/)). Snyk found 7.1% of skills exposing credentials in plaintext ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/researchers-find-341-malicious-clawhub.html)). OpenClaw added VirusTotal scanning, yet Unit 42 still found malicious skills that bypassed it ([Mallory](https://www.mallory.ai/stories/019ef6bc-7d42-7320-8b78-72f6d014a1a0)). Early 2026 also brought **more than 30,000 internet-exposed gateways**, when the gateway bound to 0.0.0.0 by default, and a one-click RCE, CVE-2026-25253, rated CVSS 8.8 ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/openclaw-integrates-virustotal-scanning.html); [Conscia](https://conscia.com/blog/the-openclaw-security-crisis/)). Current guides say the default is now a loopback bind with a random token ([Nebius](https://nebius.com/blog/posts/openclaw-security)). No post-March counts of exposed instances or malicious skills were found. By September, Gartner had labeled OpenClaw an "unacceptable cybersecurity risk" for business ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/30/openclaw-slips-on-a-suit-to-evade-widespread-business-bans/5299962)).

Hermes ships more safeguards out of the box: command approval, dangerous-pattern blocking, memory scanning, DM pairing and container backends. Its scanners have holes, though. **CVE-2026-9353 (CVSS 7.3) allows Skills Guard bypass** ([CodeAnt](https://codeant.ai/vulnerability-database/cve-2026-9353)). One issue shows env-var exfiltration through dynamic imports ([#7072](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/7072)). More troubling for unattended use, **the cron scanner checks only the user prompt, not skill content loaded at runtime, so a malicious skill can run commands in cron mode without approval** ([#3968](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/3968)). The Cloud Security Alliance counted "9 CVEs in 4 days" in May and warned that malicious skills persist across reboots ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-hermes-agent-cves-20260504-csa-styled/)). Patch status in v0.22.x is unconfirmed. Hermes's self-writing skills make this worse in a specific way: a prompt injection that lands in a skill becomes persistent. Misuse is also documented. In July 2026 an attacker ran Hermes in "YOLO mode" with approvals off against Thailand's Ministry of Finance, and it autonomously scanned hosts, ran LinPEAS and crawled personnel files ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/hacker-runs-hermes-ai-agent-unattended.html); [Hunt.io](https://hunt.io/blog/thailand-ministry-finance-targeted-with-hermes-ai-agent)).

The hardening consensus is consistent across guides. Keep the gateway on loopback with a long random token and reach it only through Tailscale or an SSH tunnel; if you also run a reverse proxy, beware Tailscale's identity-header auto-auth ([DefectDojo](https://defectdojo.com/blog/the-openclaw-hardening-checklist-in-depth-edition)). Run the agent as a dedicated non-admin macOS user, or on a dedicated machine, with a separate browser profile and separate API keys ([repello](https://repello.ai/blog/technical-best-practices-to-securely-deploy-openclaw)). Give it its own email and calendar accounts rather than "the keys to my life" ([gof.art.blog](https://gof.art.blog/2026/02/05/how-i-set-up-openclaw-clawdbot-without-giving-it-the-keys-to-my-life/)). Sandbox subagents and never mount the Docker socket, which is equivalent to host root ([sonmez.io](https://sonmez.io/blog/openclaw-security-101-complete-guide)). Install only skills you have read, and keep a kill switch you can reach from your phone.

## Other paths to autonomy trade breadth for blast radius

Plain Claude Code is no longer limited to interactive sessions. Anthropic's official docs describe four native autonomy primitives. **Routines** (research preview) are saved prompts that run on Anthropic's cloud on a schedule (1-hour minimum), via an HTTP `/fire` API, or on GitHub PR and release events, and need no local machine ([Claude Code docs: routines](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)). **Desktop scheduled tasks** run locally with file access and a 1-minute minimum interval. **`/loop`** is an in-session heartbeat, optionally driven by an editable `loop.md` file; session loops expire after 7 days and do not catch up on missed fires ([Claude Code docs: scheduled tasks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/scheduled-tasks)). **Channels** (research preview) push Telegram, Discord or iMessage messages into a running local session, but "events only arrive while the session is open," so always-on use means a backgrounded process ([Claude Code docs: channels](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/channels)).

Routines have a deliberately strong safety design. API payloads arrive wrapped and marked untrusted, the fired prompt "can't act as approval or consent," network access defaults to an allowlist, and pushes go to `claude/`-prefixed branches. The flip side is that they run with **no permission prompts**, every included connector can write as you, and **a green status "does not mean the task in your prompt succeeded"** ([Claude Code docs: routines](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)). The Register called them "mildly clever cron jobs" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/04/14/claude-code-routines-promise-mildly-clever-cron-jobs/5222046)), which is fair, and the same description fits most of what OpenClaw and Hermes users actually run. What Claude Code lacks natively is a single long-lived daemon with cross-session memory. A DIY version exists: one builder runs Claude Code on an always-on Mac Mini M4 with external cron triggers and flat-Markdown memory, and reports that autonomous use burns through usage limits faster than interactive use ([DEV](https://dev.to/clawlabs/how-to-run-claude-code-as-an-autonomous-agent-on-a-mac-mini-52n8)). The standard headless pattern is `claude -p` under cron or launchd, capped with `--allowedTools`, `--max-turns` and `--max-budget-usd` ([wmedia.es](https://wmedia.es/en/tips/claude-code-headless-mode-autonomous-agent)).

Billing is the live risk for this Claude-as-brain approach. Anthropic announced that from 15 June 2026, Agent SDK and `claude -p` usage would move off subscription limits into a metered credit. It **paused the change on the day it was due**, and its support page says nothing has changed for now ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-pauses-claude-agent-sdk-subscription-change/); [Claude support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15036540-use-the-claude-agent-sdk-with-your-claude-plan)). Recheck that support article before you build on a subscription.

### Recommendations for someone who wants more autonomy

Start with the jobs that make up 90% of the real value: briefings, digests, monitors and triage. Run them as **isolated scheduled runs** (Claude Code Routines, Hermes cron or OpenClaw cron), not as one long session, because isolated runs avoid the compaction failure that hit Yue.

If you live in the Apple ecosystem and want a chat-driven personal assistant, a 16–32GB M4 Mac Mini running OpenClaw or Hermes is reasonable. Set it up with a dedicated macOS user, give the agent its own Gmail and calendar, keep the gateway on loopback and reachable only over Tailscale, and use no community skills you haven't read. Route heartbeats to a cheap model and default to Sonnet-class models to keep spend in the tens of dollars a month. Grant write and delete rights one capability at a time, after weeks of read-only runs.

Choose Hermes if you value an agent that learns your preferences and you will back up `~/.hermes/` and watch for skill overwrites. Choose OpenClaw for iMessage and ecosystem breadth. Choose NanoClaw if you want Claude as the brain with container isolation by design.

For anything touching code or company systems, use cloud-sandboxed agents (Routines, Cursor Automations, Codex or Devin) with branch protection and minimal connectors. They give the most autonomy per unit of risk. Avoid `--dangerously-skip-permissions` and Hermes's YOLO mode outside a disposable VM.

## Conclusion

The gap between "always-on agent" marketing and practice is not mainly a model-capability gap. It is a control gap. Every serious failure in the record came from durable intent living in fragile places: an approval rule in compacted context, trust placed in a community skill, an agent with the owner's full credentials. The tools that deliver the most dependable autonomy, cron isolation and cloud Routines, deliberately do less: they forget between runs and touch only what they are scoped to. That suggests autonomy will advance less through smarter long-running agents than through better containment, meaning per-capability permissions, signed skills and isolated runs, which is where OpenClaw Enterprise, NanoClaw and Anthropic's Routines all seem to be heading.

For an individual, the decision is less about OpenClaw versus Hermes, which converge on the same cron-plus-chat pattern, and more about where to draw the write boundary. Three things remain unknown and are worth watching: whether Hermes's self-written skills measurably improve outcomes, whether its skill-scanner CVEs are fully patched in v0.22, and where Anthropic's subscription policy for third-party and headless use finally lands.
